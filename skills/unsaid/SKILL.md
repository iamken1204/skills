# Unsaid

Calibration: **"We build structures that hold."** Holds what? Unsaid — that's the trick. The sentence is definite; the referent isn't. The whole style is that one move repeated: **say the sentence fully, leave the referent unsaid**. The reader fills in what gets held — a business, a product, a person's trust — and readers persuade themselves better than copy can.

Canon: SSI's one-page letter, early Anduril, New Computer, OpenAI's charter-era pages.

## The sentence

- Concrete verb, abstract object. The verb commits ("build", "hold", "run"); the object stays a bare noun the reader can inhabit ("structures", "systems", "machines").
- Declarative present tense, delivered as fact. Plain words carry the confidence — a hype adjective ("innovative", "cutting-edge") or an exclamation mark spends it.
- Beliefs over benefits: "We believe X", never "X helps you Y".

## The scope

Scope refusal is the discipline: the copy never names the market, the customer, or the feature list. "For e-commerce SMBs" collapses the mystery into a category — the one unrecoverable error. Where normal copy would specify, substitute the abstract object; where it would enumerate deliverables, allow at most three bare nouns.

## The medium

- Manifesto over marketing, whatever the container: beliefs, short lines, heavy whitespace. The style fits anywhere copy would otherwise sell — a landing page, a mission statement, a bio, a launch post, a tagline.
- Subtraction as confidence: saying almost nothing implies you don't need to persuade — which reads as strength. When trimming, cut whole sections before weakening sentences. On a page, subtraction extends to the chrome: often no navigation at all.

## Check

Done when every sentence passes the first two tests and the piece passes the rest:

1. **Definite** — declarative present tense, concrete verb, zero hype adjectives, zero hedges.
2. **Unsaid** — three different readers could each claim a different referent. A sentence only one kind of reader could claim says too much: abstract the object or cut the sentence.
3. **Scoped to nothing** — no market, no customer type, no deliverable beyond bare nouns (three at most).
4. **No tells** — sweep the finished copy for word-level AI tells: stock vocabulary ("delve", "tapestry", "testament", "seamless", "robust", "landscape", "leverage", "elevate", "journey"), filler openers ("it's important to note", "in today's world"), vague attributions ("experts believe"), and superficial -ing trailers ("…, highlighting the importance of X"). The unsaid referent, the portent, and the three-noun triad are the style, not tells — keep them even if they look flagged.

To brief a designer in one phrase: "manifesto site with strategic ambiguity."
